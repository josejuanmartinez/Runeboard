I · The board

A living tabletop

Middle-earth is laid out as one continuous painted map, every hex painted by hand, and on it your war is played with miniatures — figures on their bases, towns built hex by hex, fog that rolls back as your scouts ride out.

Miniatures, not sprites

Every hero and captain is a live, animated 3D figure on a painted base in its nation's colour. They walk the roads, raise their guard, cast, fall — and your own company stands out at a glance.

171 settlements, each its own

Every town, hold and ruin has its own painting — and its own miniature built from it: Imladris' falls and domed halls, the seven circles of Minas Tirith, Hobbiton's smials, the black horns of Orthanc.

A board that breathes

Cloud shadows drift over the land, chimneys smoke, birds cross the map. The seasons turn with the calendar, dusk falls while rival nations move, and the camera leans in on a duel or a battle.

Each settlement is painted by hand — and rebuilt on the board as its own miniature from that painting.

II · The war

Three fronts, one fate

Lead a wizard, a dark lord or a neutral power through an age of the War of the Ring. Every leader brings its own deck, its own realm and its own way to win.

The Free Peoples

Kindle hope.

Rally the scattered realms of Elves, Dwarves, Men and Hobbits, hold the White City and the Last Homely House, and carry the war to the Mountain of Fire.

The Shadow

Cover the lands.

Unleash vast hosts, Ringwraiths and terror, and break the strongholds of the West one by one before the age runs out.

The Neutral Powers

Master the board.

Scheme, industrialise and play both sides — strike where the great powers are weakest and take the board for yourself.

III · The cards

Every hero, host and hold is a card

Characters, armies, lands, settlements, artifacts, spells and encounters — hundreds of illustrated cards, each one a piece of the board. Build your hand around your leader and play the moment: a duel, a march, a spell, an omen.

IV · A turn of the age

Every move writes history

An age lasts sixty days. Each turn is one of them: muster, march, scheme, fight — then the calendar turns and the other nations move in the dusk.

  1. Survey your realm

    Your capital, characters and resources wait on the board. Fog hides the rest of Middle-earth until you explore it.

  2. Answer events

    Encounters hide in the hexes and omens arrive with the dawn — illustrated moments that ask you to choose.

  3. Fight for the ground

    Armies clash and heroes duel. Hold strongholds, defend the roads, chase the objectives of your age.

  4. Close the day

    End your turn and watch the rival powers answer — every march, broken oath and last stand logged as it happens.

V · The minds behind the Shadow

A war that thinks back

Every rival nation and every character it commands is played by a layered AI — not a script. It reads the board, remembers what it has seen, sets itself missions, plans how to achieve them, and checks its own judgement against a neural network trained on good play. Turn on Watch Mode and the whole war plays itself.

Utility AI

Every nation constantly scores danger, opportunity, growth and stagnation — literally everything that can be quantified about the board — to weigh what is worth doing next.

Watch Mode

Hand your own nation to the AI and watch the age play out: Gandalf rallies the West, Saruman schemes, the Dark Lord's hosts pour from the East. A good way to learn the game — and to see the minds at work.

VI · The art

Painted, not prompted

Runeboard's retro look started on paper: old black-and-white illustrations from RPG books, redrawn and hand-painted with the colours and techniques of the 1980s — then taught to an open-weight model so the cards could share that one hand.

  1. Scan & redraw

    Old black-and-white illustrations, scanned and redrawn.

  2. Paint by hand

    Twenty-five of them painted with 80s palettes and techniques.

  3. Teach a model

    A LoRA on the open-weight Qwen Image model, trained only on that art, paints the cards.

  4. Repaint the land

    Hex tiles built from Unity Asset Store pieces, repainted by hand into one map.
